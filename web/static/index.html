About Matrix Watcher

Mission

Matrix Watcher monitors eleven streams eligible for anomaly detection, one calculated earth-tide covariate and four context feeds. It asks whether unusual observations across distinct domains coincide more often than timing controls suggest. No cross-domain discovery or reliable prediction has been independently validated.

Data Sources

These eleven anomaly streams and the earth-tide covariate have different update schedules. The coverage panel shows the latest freshness and completeness; a green site connection alone does not certify the measurements.

Four context feeds

NASA's fireball catalogue can arrive days after an event. RIPE Atlas compares K-root and F-root pings from the same fixed public probe panel. The Weather Grid shows six model estimates from Open-Meteo. Global Stations shows physical airport METAR reports from New York, Riga, São Paulo, Cape Town, Tokyo and Sydney, including the time each observation was made. All four feeds expose gaps and add context; none raises the live anomaly-cluster level. The six airports share one atmospheric domain and one reporting API.

How an anomaly is detected

Continuous measurements use rolling robust statistics where appropriate. Named events such as significant earthquakes and geomagnetic storms use explicit physical thresholds. News bursts use a fixed count of newly seen headlines. An unavailable feed is a coverage problem, never a normal reading.

Can this predict the next earthquake?

No. USGS events are observed after they occur. A legacy pattern tracker counted past M6+ events after other conditions and recorded where matched events had happened. The same quake could appear in successive hourly-aggregate polls, so those counts are not independent quake outcomes. Its most frequent past region was never tested as an advance location forecast. The tracker no longer updates or emits those seismic associations as predictions. A credible test would freeze a geographic region, magnitude threshold, time window and probability before future events, then compare every hit and miss with a seismic base-rate model and independent future data. This site is not an earthquake warning service.

Coincidences across domains

When anomalies fall in the same 30-second window, the level counts distinct domains, rather than the number of feeds. Solar wind, solar activity and geomagnetic Kp count as one heliophysics domain. A cluster is a temporal coincidence, not evidence of causation or statistical significance. Polling intervals and upstream reporting delays can make a short window miss real events. The Observation Atlas lets you compare sampling opportunity for hypothetical 30-second, 5-minute, 15-minute and 1-hour events; these bars are not detector probabilities.

Which time does a record mean?

New anomaly records distinguish source event time, feed publication time, collector observation time and detector time when those values exist. Missing upstream times stay unknown. USGS earthquake origin is not when the collector first saw it; a weekly volcano report date is not eruption onset. The legacy historical archive is not backfilled with invented times.

What changed, and what to expect

Repairs to collection, anomaly processing and coverage checks improve the chance that the system records an event it previously missed. They do not make unexplained phenomena more common. The global weather stations and other context feeds make the site more informative but do not create extra anomaly votes. A quiet day remains inconclusive when a source was missing, late or sampled too slowly.

From patterns to tested signals

Historical condition → event frequencies are exploratory descriptions, not calibrated forecast probabilities. Evidence Lab compares three coincidence windows with shifted-time controls; Observation Atlas measures sampling opportunity; Lag Lab tests twelve directed lag comparisons. A frozen 120-day lag screen and a five-rule Forecast Audit cover 24 September 2026 through 22 January 2027 UTC. A separate future observation-time coincidence screen covers 25 September 2026 through 23 January 2027 UTC. It excludes weekly volcanic reports and days with inadequate source coverage, does not issue interim p-values, and still requires independent replication. A candidate is only eligible for the public signal panel after separate future-data evaluation:

  • Freeze the event definition, horizon and candidate before evaluating future data
  • Count independent event episodes and compare against a current, matched base rate
  • Report misses, uncertainty and calibration; account for the number of candidates tried
  • Until those checks pass, no forecast is displayed

How We Avoid Fooling Ourselves

We preserve raw observations, audit sensor coverage and use replay and within-month whole-day shifts of domain event trains as timing controls. Historical exploratory tests do not establish a discovery. The forecast diary scores a negative outcome only when target coverage is adequate.

  • Edge-triggering: one ongoing event counts once, not once per poll
  • Timing control: compare real episodes with shifted domain event trains
  • Future-data validation: required before a candidate becomes a public signal
  • Matched base rate: a frequent event alone is not a finding

What We've Found So Far

No cross-domain predictive edge has been independently verified. A September 2026 audit found historical coverage gaps and a processing error. Fixes improve future collection but cannot fill missing historical observations. In the available post-May-2026 history, the 30-second research window had no three-domain episode; wider-window coincidences were inconclusive after controls. The frozen future-data period ends on 22 January 2027 UTC and requires endpoint review.

What the numbers mean

Detection uses transparent statistical rules, not a generative model. A cluster level is a domain count, not a p-value. Historical condition frequencies are sample proportions and should not be read as reliable forecast probabilities:

  • Evidence Lab's historical p-values are exploratory and corrected across its three windows
  • Forecast Audit records candidate probabilities before outcomes and scores them against a fixed comparison
  • Even a promising future score needs endpoint review and independent replication

Philosophy

Matrix Watcher does not claim to know why a coincidence occurs. It records what it can observe, exposes gaps in coverage, and compares timing against controls. An empty result remains inconclusive when measurements are incomplete or the test has low power.
